# Figure legends

## Figure 7 | Combined N-inh5 and RHPS4 treatment induces promoter-centred G4 remodelling and transcriptional rewiring

**a,** Mean BG4 CUT&Tag signal across ±10 kb of 35,143 gene-level transcription start sites in DMSO-, RHPS4-, N-inh5- and combination-treated GBM-PDC2 cells. Condition-specific profiles and ranked heatmaps are shown below the combined profile. **b,** Genome-wide distribution of the consensus G4 landscape and differential G4 peaks. The three point tracks show RHPS4, N-inh5 and combination treatment relative to DMSO; red and blue indicate gained and lost peaks at FDR < 0.05. Triangles show factorial RHPS4 × N-inh5 interaction peaks on standard chromosomes. **c,** Direct combination-versus-DMSO G4 and RNA effects for 1,577 genes measured in both datasets. Each gene is represented by one G4 peak; selected opposite-direction loci are labelled and coloured by peak annotation. **d,** Left, proportion of peaks containing a predicted G4 motif (pqsfinder score ≥ 40). Motifs occurred in 20 of 42 interaction peaks and 536 of 2,000 width-matched random regions (two-sided Fisher exact P = 0.004529); the promoter-interaction and full consensus bars are descriptive. Right, interaction direction after defining promoter proximity as overlap with a symmetric ±3-kb window around the supplied gene-level TSS coordinates. Six peaks were promoter-proximal and all had positive interaction coefficients (6 of 21 positive versus 0 of 21 negative; two-sided Fisher exact P = 0.02069). **e,** Left, replicate-level BG4 signal relative to DMSO at 40 interaction-peak display loci. Columns are annotated by genomic location and interaction direction. Right, replicate and mean BG4 signal at the BAP1 promoter-proximal peak (chr3:52,406,264–52,407,442; hg38). **f,** RNA response of selected promoter-interaction loci, direct G4–RNA candidates and p21, DREAM/cell-cycle and senescence genes. Values are standardized within each gene across DMSO, RHPS4, N-inh5 and combination treatment.

## Supplementary Figure 5 | Independent genetic screens identify BAP1 loss as a potential sensitizer to G4-targeting compounds

**a,** Fraction of BAP1-targeting shRNAs significantly depleted during pyridostatin treatment in three independent screens. Depletion was observed for 4 of 8 shRNAs in the A375 genome-wide screen, 7 of 7 in the A375 focused screen and 4 of 7 in the HT1080 focused screen. The dashed line marks 50% of tested shRNAs. **b,** BAP1 knockout response across 31 CRISPR–drug screens ordered by normalized Z score (normZ). Negative values indicate increased drug sensitivity after BAP1 knockout. Phen-DC3 produced the strongest response (normZ = −5.81; BAP1 rank 7 of 17,298 genes; negative-direction DrugZ FDR = 7.87 × 10⁻⁶). Pyridostatin produced the third-strongest response (normZ = −2.56; BAP1 rank 163 of 17,374 genes), but did not pass multiple-testing correction (negative-direction DrugZ FDR = 0.552).
